SafeSpace

Analytical Design Fiction · Critical HCI · 2026

What if an AI companion was designed to become less necessary over time?

Moving to a new country can mean navigating unfamiliar systems, distant relationships and moments of loneliness. In these situations, AI conversations can become accessible spaces for reflection: always available, seemingly attentive, and free from the obligations of human interaction.

But what kind of care can a machine offer when it cannot participate in human relationships?

SafeSpace explores this question through the experiences of recently arrived Iranian women in Germany. It examines how AI-mediated emotional support is understood and negotiated alongside human relationships and community-based support.

Three spaces between the self and others

  1. 01 / Reflect. A private space for AI-assisted journaling, articulating thoughts and navigating uncertainty.
  2. 02 / Recognise. Anonymous experiences from other women, making connection possible through recognition rather than direct disclosure.
  3. 03 / Reach out. A directory of counselling, legal and integration resources, extending support beyond the interface.

The research behind the fiction

Eight narrative interviews with Farsi-speaking Iranian women in their first two years in Germany revealed five tensions in how AI-mediated support is experienced. SafeSpace does not resolve them, and it does not attempt to solve loneliness through technology. It makes these tensions tangible, and asks what role AI should occupy in moments of vulnerability and transition.

Five tensions, no easy answers

  • Orientationuncertainty
  • Expressionself-protection
  • Digital clarityrelational depth
  • Being rememberedbeing tracked
  • Warmthartificiality
